First sort what people found

The sightings show many kinds of life

The dots gather into groups such as plants and birds. They also show insects and mammals. Fungi and reptiles appear too. One shared photo becomes part of a larger picture.

What did everyone find?

People recorded thousands of different living things

The dots now gather by taxon. A taxon is the name used to identify a living thing. The project contains 3,120 taxon IDs.

The record grows

More sightings joined the record each year

The first sighting was shared in 2002. The biggest rise began in 2025. People shared 3,570 sightings that year. The total for 2024 was 298. By September 2026 people had already shared 4,744 more. I am excited to see what future visits will reveal.

Who shared the sightings?

Ninety-two people shaped this record

Ninety-two people contributed to this project. They visited different places and noticed different forms of life. Each person recorded something that others might have missed.

Bring the records back together

Their separate sightings become one shared map

All 10,461 dots now return to the map. No single person recorded every place or every kind of life. Each person added what they saw. Citizen science joins those records into a view that everyone can learn from.

Look more closely at the taxa

The Canopy of Life

Each of the 3,120 leaves represents one taxon ID. The 14 large branches show broad groups of life. Smaller branches connect taxa that share a genus name. Colour identifies the group. Leaf size shows the number of sightings. Hover over a leaf or select it to learn more.

Leaf size shows how often a taxon appears in this project. It does not show how common that organism is in the forest.

How often was each taxon seen?

Most taxa were seen only once or twice

Most taxon IDs appear in only one or two sightings. A smaller group appears many times. Only 31% were seen at least three times. Yet they make up 75% of all sightings. Only nine taxon IDs were seen 50 times or more.

taxa with one or two sightings

A taxon seen once may not be rare. It may be hard to find. It is also possible that no one looked again.

Here “taxon” means one distinct taxon ID in the export. It does not always mean a species.

Where did people go?

What citizen science can do

One sighting can become
shared knowledge

This project contains 10,461 sightings and 3,120 taxon IDs. The records came from 92 people. Each record began with a simple act. Someone noticed a living thing and chose to share it.

Citizen science cannot show everything. It shows what people noticed. It also shows what they could record and share.

Behind every dot

Every dot began when someone met a living thing

A sighting may look like one small mark on a screen. It began with a real encounter. A person stopped to look. They took a photo and shared what they found.

These photographs come from iNaturalist records that allow reuse. Credits and licenses are shown with each image.
